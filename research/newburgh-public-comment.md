# Newburgh City Council — Public Comment (≈2 minutes)

*For After Incarceration. Read at a City Council meeting / public-comment period.
Verified facts only; sources in `newburgh-flock-dossier.md`. Swap [brackets] as needed.*

---

Good evening. My name is [name], with After Incarceration.

Newburgh has called itself a **Safe and Welcoming City**. This Council reaffirmed that
commitment. But we are running a network of **more than 80 Flock surveillance cameras** that
photograph and log every vehicle that passes — residents, workers, families — whether or not
anyone is suspected of anything.

Here is the problem with that, in plain terms. Flock's cameras don't stand alone; they sit on
a **national network**. Independent audits have documented federal and out-of-state agencies
reaching local Flock data three ways — direct sharing, back-door searches that show up in
audit logs, and local officers running searches **on behalf of** ICE. Nationally, that's
already happened **thousands of times.**

So you cannot, at the same time, promise sanctuary **and** operate cameras that ICE can reach.
One of those commitments is not real. And right now, **no one in this room can prove which one.**

We paid Flock **$274,400 in 2024** — with state grant money. We're told cancelling costs
around **$400,000**. Those are real numbers. But the deeper cost is this: a city that
surveils everyone, with no public proof of who's looking at the data.

So three asks tonight:

1. **Take the recorded vote.** A resolution to end this contract has been brought three times
   and never voted on. The public deserves to see where each of you stands.
2. **Show the audit logs** — before any "we'll just add safeguards" vote. Prove no outside or
   federal agency has searched Newburgh's cameras. If you can't prove it, you don't control it.
3. **Disclose the data-sharing settings** — exactly which agencies and states our cameras
   feed.

A sanctuary city keeps its promises in public, on the record. Thank you.

---

*Delivery tips: speak slowly, look up at the "no one can prove which one" line, and end on
the three asks. If there's a 3-minute limit, this fits with room to spare.*
