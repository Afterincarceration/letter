*Compiled June 2026. Sources cited inline. "OPEN" = still needs a primary document.*
*Prepared for transparency/advocacy use. Figures are from public reporting unless a
primary document is cited; verify against records obtained via FOIL.*

---

## 1. Executive summary

The City of Newburgh, NY contracts with **Flock Safety** for **80+ automated
license-plate-reader (ALPR) cameras**. The city paid Flock **$274,400 in 2024** under a
roughly **two-year contract**, funded by a **New York State DCJS grant**. Cancelling
mid-contract would cost **~$400,000**. Council Member **Omari Shakur** has introduced a
cancellation resolution **three times with no vote**. The central objection: Newburgh is a
**"Safe and Welcoming" (sanctuary) city**, but Flock's data-sharing architecture creates a
plausible pathway for **ICE / federal access** to plate data.

## 4. Core issue: sanctuary city vs. ICE access

Newburgh's sanctuary policy limits cooperation with federal immigration enforcement, but
Flock's national network creates documented access paths that can bypass it:

- **Front door:** Newburgh shares data 1:1 with agencies that cooperate with ICE.
- **Back door:** audits show federal searches even where sharing wasn't explicitly enabled.
- **Side door:** local officers run searches *on behalf of* ICE. **4,000+** such
  immigration-related lookups have been reported nationally.

Implication: unless data-sharing is provably **off** and audited, a sanctuary city's own
cameras can feed federal enforcement. This is why "just add safeguards" (the Chief/PD
position) is contested — the burden is on the city to *prove* no external access.

## 7. Flock's general policy vs. Newburgh specifics

Flock's *defaults* (not necessarily Newburgh's settings):
- Data retention ~**30 days** (adjustable by customer law/policy).
- Customer "owns" the data; customer chooses whether to share.

**These defaults do not establish Newburgh's actual configuration.** Retention length,
the sharing list, and any federal-access history must come from the contract + audit logs
(see FOIL below).

## 9. Public-comment / advocacy talking points

- **Sanctuary contradiction:** "Newburgh promised to be a Safe and Welcoming City. A
  surveillance network with documented federal back-doors breaks that promise — you can't
  be a sanctuary and run cameras ICE can search."
- **Burden of proof on the city:** "Before any 'safeguards' vote, show us the audit logs
  proving no outside or federal agency has searched Newburgh's cameras."
- **Cost framing:** "We paid $274,400 in 2024 — with state grant money. The question isn't
  only the cancellation cost; it's whether dragnet surveillance of every resident is worth
  it at all."
- **Grant ≠ free:** "DCJS grant funding doesn't make this consequence-free — the data risks
  and civil-liberties costs land on Newburgh residents, not Albany."
- **Justice-impacted lens:** "Communities already over-policed bear the brunt of dragnet
  surveillance and its errors — including the misplaced camera already found in the city's
  own data."
- **The ask:** a recorded up-or-down vote on cancellation; full public disclosure of
  sharing settings; an independent audit.
